Water heater guide

Water heater: repair or replace?

Water heaters have two things no other appliance shares: the shortest typical lifespan, and the highest energy stakes on the electric side. Both push the decision harder on age — and a leaking tank ends the debate entirely.

Data reviewed July 19, 2026 · BLS OEWS 49-9031 · EIA residential rates

If the tank is sound and you’ve lost hot water, the fix is usually cheap: an electric heating element or thermostat, both relatively DIY-friendly; on gas, a valve or burner that needs a pro. But water heaters age fast (9–12 years mid-range), run up real electricity bills, and fail in a way that risks water damage — so the age of the unit does more work here than in any other category.

A leaking tank means replace

Once the tank body corrodes and leaks, there is no part to swap — and a failing tank can flood the space around it. That’s a replacement, and often an urgent one. Gas valve and burner repairs are professional-only safety hard-stops.

Energy is a real factor here — especially electric

An aging electric water heater draws roughly 4,200 kWh/year versus about 3,200 kWh for a modern one — a ~1,000 kWh saving worth about $178/year nationally and more in high-rate states. A new gas unit saves around 30 therms/year (~$40). Note that installation is significant — roughly $800–$900 — so the calculator weighs that in too.

Before paying for any repair, run a federal recall check — a recall remedy is usually free.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a water heater last?

A conventional tank water heater lasts about 6–9 years at the budget tier, 9–12 mid-range, and 12–18 premium. These are among the shortest lifespans of any major appliance, so age matters a lot in the decision.

Should I repair or replace a leaking water heater?

A leaking tank almost always means replace. Once the tank itself corrodes and leaks, there’s no serviceable part to fix — and a failed tank can cause water damage. Repairs like a heating element or thermostat only make sense when the tank is sound.

How much does it cost to fix a water heater with no hot water?

On an electric unit, a failed heating element ($150–$350) or thermostat ($120–$260) is the usual cause, and both are relatively DIY-friendly. On a gas unit, a gas valve ($200–$350) or burner ($180–$400) is common and must be handled by a licensed professional.

Do energy savings justify replacing an old electric water heater?

They can. An aging electric water heater uses roughly 4,200 kWh/year versus about 3,200 kWh for a modern one — a ~1,000 kWh saving worth around $178/year at the national average rate, and more in high-rate states. Over a horizon that adds up and can tip an old, marginal unit toward replacement.
