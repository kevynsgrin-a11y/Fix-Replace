Cost guides

Know before you get the quote.

Each guide gives you the same evidence base our calculator runs on: how long the appliance is built to last by brand tier, what commonly fails and what it typically costs to fix, and a clear rule for when repairing beats replacing. Real figures, sourced from NAHB, InterNACHI, BLS, and EIA data.

Every guide ends with a link into the calculator, so you can run your exact unit, age and quote for a personalized verdict.

Pick your appliance

Refrigerators

Compressors, sealed systems, and evaporator fans — the priciest failures to weigh.

Mid-tier lifespan 11–15 yrs

Washing machines

Drain pumps, bearings, and transmissions across front- and top-load designs.

Mid-tier lifespan 10–14 yrs

Dishwashers

Drain pumps, control boards, and door seals — often DIY-friendly repairs.

Mid-tier lifespan 9–12 yrs

Dryers

Thermal fuses and heating elements are cheap; bearings and gas valves aren’t.

Mid-tier lifespan 11–14 yrs

Ranges

Gas and electric — igniters, elements, and boards, with gas work strictly pro-only.

Mid-tier lifespan 12–17 yrs

Wall ovens

Bake elements, igniters, and boards — plus a high install cost that shifts the math.

Mid-tier lifespan 13–17 yrs

Water heaters

Elements, gas valves, and burners — and big energy stakes over the horizon.

Mid-tier lifespan 9–12 yrs

Microwaves

Over-the-range units where every internal repair is a high-voltage, pro-only job.

Mid-tier lifespan 8–10 yrs

How to read the “repair vs replace” sections

Every guide gives an appliance-specific rule that beats the old “50% rule” by folding in age and repeat-failure risk. For the full model — the Weibull remaining-life curve and the Net Present Cost comparison — see how it works.
