Dryer guide

Dryer: repair or replace?

Dryers are mechanically simple, and the most common failures — a no-heat fault — are among the cheapest repairs in the house. The exceptions are a worn drum bearing and, on gas models, any burner work, which is strictly professional.

Data reviewed July 19, 2026 · BLS OEWS 49-9031 · EIA residential rates

A dryer that tumbles but won’t heat is usually a blown thermal fuse, a failed heating element, or a bad thermostat — each an inexpensive, homeowner-serviceable part. That’s why dryers skew heavily toward “repair.” The decision only tightens when the machine needs a drum bearing, or when a gas model’s valve or burner fails and a licensed pro is required.

Gas components are a safety hard-stop

Gas valve and burner work carries fire, explosion, and carbon-monoxide risk. On gas dryers, these repairs are professional-only — we never provide DIY parts or tutorials for them.

When to repair vs when to replace

SituationLean
No-heat fault (fuse, element, thermostat) at almost any ageRepair
Control board on a unit within its lifespan bandRepair
Drum bearing on a machine past ~11 yearsWeigh replacing
Gas valve/burner on an out-of-band budget dryerUsually replace

In major metros a thermal-fuse replacement runs around $145 — a fraction of a new $900 dryer, so fixing it is a clear win. The reason to replace is almost always a stacked case: a labor-heavy repair, an aged machine, and the roughly $340 ($180 install on gas) it costs to deliver, install, and haul away a new one all pointing the same direction.

Energy: modest, and only on electric

An efficient electric dryer saves roughly 300 kWh/year (about $53/year nationally) versus an aging one. Gas dryers’ energy gap is tiny. Useful, but the repair economics lead.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a clothes dryer last?

A dryer lasts about 8–11 years at the budget tier, 11–14 mid-range, and 14–18 premium. Dryers are mechanically simple, so many reach the top of their band with basic maintenance.

Is a dryer that won’t heat worth fixing?

Almost always. A no-heat dryer is usually a thermal fuse ($100–$190 installed), a heating element ($120–$280), or a thermostat ($120–$260) — all inexpensive and DIY-friendly on an electric dryer. These repairs are worth doing even on an older machine.

Should I repair a gas dryer’s gas valve myself?

No. A gas valve ($200–$350) and gas burner ($180–$400) are safety hard-stops because of fire and carbon-monoxide risk. This work must be done by a licensed professional — we never show DIY tutorials for gas components.

When does it make sense to replace a dryer instead of repairing it?

When the repair is a labor-heavy job like a drum bearing ($250–$450) on a machine past its lifespan band and nearing the price of a new one (about $900 mid-range), replacement usually wins. Cheap heating and control faults on almost any age dryer favor repair.
