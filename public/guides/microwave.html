Microwave guide

Microwave: repair or replace?

The microwave is the one appliance where the answer is nearly always “replace” — not because it can’t be fixed, but because fixing it must be done by a professional and costs a large slice of a cheap new unit.

Two facts decide almost every microwave case. First, every internal repair is a high-voltage safety hard-stop — the capacitor can hold a lethal charge even unplugged — so there is no DIY path. Second, a new over-the-range unit is inexpensive: about $200 budget or $350 mid-range. When a pro magnetron repair runs $150–$300, it’s hard for repair to win.

Every internal microwave repair is high-voltage

Regardless of which part failed, we treat any internal microwave repair as a high-voltage safety hard-stop and route it to a professional. Microwave capacitors store a dangerous charge even when the unit is unplugged. See the safety hard-stops.

The main exception is a premium or built-in microwave still under warranty, where the manufacturer may cover the repair — always check. Outside that, the math is stark: a $250 professional repair on a $350 appliance, with another failure likely soon on an aging unit, rarely beats a fresh replacement (and its modest ~$330 in install, delivery, and haul-away).

Energy isn’t a factor

Microwaves are efficient and used briefly; the difference between old and new is roughly 20 kWh/year — a couple of dollars. This decision is purely about repair economics and safety.

Frequently asked questions

How long does an over-the-range microwave last?

An over-the-range microwave lasts about 5–8 years at the budget tier, 8–10 mid-range, and 10–14 premium — the shortest lifespan among common kitchen appliances.

Is it worth repairing a microwave?

Usually not. A magnetron repair runs $150–$300 by a professional, and a new over-the-range microwave is only about $200 budget or $350 mid-range. Because the repair must be done by a pro (high-voltage) and costs a large share of a new unit, replacement usually wins — especially past the 5–8 year budget lifespan.

Why can’t I repair a microwave myself?

Every internal microwave repair is treated as a high-voltage safety hard-stop. Microwave capacitors can hold a lethal charge even after the unit is unplugged, so we never show DIY parts or tutorials for internal microwave work — it’s professional-only.

My microwave sparks or hums but won’t heat — repair or replace?

A no-heat microwave is usually a failed magnetron ($150–$300 professionally). Given a new unit costs $200–$350 and the repair is pro-only, replacement is almost always the rational choice unless the unit is a pricey premium built-in still within warranty.
