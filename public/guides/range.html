Range guide

Range: repair or replace?

Ranges are among the longest-lived appliances in the home, which tilts most repairs toward “fix it.” The dividing line is fuel: electric elements are easy DIY parts, while every gas repair is a professional, safety-critical job.

A range that won’t heat one element or has a dead oven is usually a cheap, targeted fix — a bake element, an igniter, a thermostat. Because a mid-range unit runs $1,100–$1,200 new and lasts 12–17 years, the economics strongly favor repair right up until a major fault lands on an end-of-life unit. Gas and electric diverge on who does the work, not on the underlying math.

Gas work is professional-only

Igniters, valves, and burners on a gas range carry fire, explosion, and carbon-monoxide risk. These are safety hard-stops — a licensed pro only, never DIY. See the safety hard-stops.

Energy barely moves this decision

A modern range saves only about 80 kWh/year (electric) or a few therms (gas) over an older one — a handful of dollars annually. Unlike refrigerators or water heaters, efficiency is not a reason to replace a range.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a range last?

A gas range lasts about 10–14 years budget, 13–17 mid-range, and 20–25 premium. Electric ranges run about 9–13 years budget, 12–16 mid-range, and 18–22 premium. Ranges are among the longest-lived kitchen appliances.

Is it worth fixing an electric range’s bake element?

Yes. A bake or broil element is $120–$280 installed and DIY-friendly, and ranges last a long time, so on a unit inside its lifespan band this is a clear repair. Thermostats ($120–$260) and control boards ($200–$500) are similar.

Why can’t I replace a gas range igniter myself?

Gas igniters ($150–$300), gas valves ($200–$350), and burner assemblies ($180–$400) are safety hard-stops because of fire and carbon-monoxide risk. This work must be done by a licensed professional; we never show DIY parts or tutorials for gas components.

When should I replace a range instead of repairing it?

Because ranges are long-lived and a new one costs $1,100–$1,200 mid-range plus delivery and install, most single repairs favor fixing. Replacement makes sense mainly for a major gas fault on a unit well past its lifespan band, or when multiple systems are failing at once.
