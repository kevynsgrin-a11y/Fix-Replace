Wall oven guide

Wall oven: repair or replace?

A built-in wall oven is the appliance where the install cost changes everything. Because a new one has to be fitted into the cabinetry, replacement is pricey — which pushes the rational answer toward repair far more often than for a plug-in appliance.

Wall ovens are long-lived (13–17 years mid-range) and expensive to swap out: a new unit runs $900–$3,500, and installation alone is around $300 because it’s built in. Add delivery and haul-away and you’re looking at roughly $520 in costs beyond the oven itself. That’s why even a $500 control board usually beats replacing a working-but-faulted oven.

The install cost is the story

For most appliances a high repair quote pushes you toward buying new. Wall ovens flip that instinct: the ~$520 it costs just to get a new one delivered, installed, and hauled away means a lot of repair is “cheaper than the setup fee” for a replacement. When in doubt, repair.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a wall oven last?

A wall oven lasts about 10–14 years at the budget tier, 13–17 mid-range, and 18–23 premium.

Why do wall ovens lean toward repair more than other appliances?

Because replacing a built-in wall oven is expensive: the unit runs $900–$3,500 and installation alone is around $300, since it’s fitted into cabinetry. That high replacement cost means most single repairs — even a $500 control board — are the rational choice while the oven is inside its lifespan band.

How much does it cost to fix an oven that won’t heat?

On an electric oven, a bad bake or broil element is $120–$280 installed and DIY-friendly. A thermostat is $120–$260 and a control board $200–$500. On a gas oven, an igniter ($150–$300) is the usual culprit and must be handled by a professional.

Is a gas oven igniter a DIY job?

No. Gas igniters ($150–$300) and gas valves ($200–$350) are safety hard-stops due to fire and carbon-monoxide risk and must be done by a licensed professional.
