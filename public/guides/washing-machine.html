Washing machine guide

Washing machine: repair or replace?

Most washer faults are cheap, DIY-friendly, and worth fixing. The exceptions — a transmission or a drum bearing — are labor-heavy jobs that, on an older machine, tip the scales toward a new one.

Data reviewed July 19, 2026 · BLS OEWS 49-9031 · EIA residential rates

A washer that won’t drain or fills slowly is usually a $150–$320 pump or a $120–$250 inlet valve — an afternoon’s work and an easy “repair.” The decision only gets interesting when the machine develops a deep mechanical fault: a worn transmission or a failed drum bearing, both of which mean significant professional labor. Front-load and top-load designs age a little differently, so we break out both.

When to repair vs when to replace

SituationLean
Pump, inlet valve, or board on a machine within its lifespan bandRepair
Transmission or bearing on a front-load past ~10 yearsUsually replace
Any repair under ~$250 on a top-load under ~11 yearsRepair
A $500+ mechanical quote on a budget machine near end-of-lifeReplace

The pivot is mechanical labor on an aged machine. A $500 transmission on a 12-year-old top-load is more than half the price of a new $900 unit — and once you add the odds of a second failure and roughly $320 in delivery, install, and haul-away, replacing is often the rational call. The same $500 on a 4-year-old machine is an easy repair.

Energy is a minor factor here

A modern washer saves only about 90 kWh/year in machine electricity (roughly $16/year nationally), because most of a wash’s energy is the hot water your water heater provides. Don’t let efficiency alone drive a washer decision — the repair economics dominate.

Before paying for any repair, run a federal recall check — a recall remedy is usually free.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a washing machine last?

A front-load washer lasts about 7–10 years at the budget tier, 10–13 mid-range, and 15–20 premium. Top-load washers run about 8–11 years budget, 11–14 mid-range, and 15–25 premium.

Is it worth replacing the transmission or bearings in a washer?

A transmission runs $350–$550 and a drum bearing or spider $250–$450, both professional jobs. On a machine past its mid-range lifespan and near the price of a new one ($900 top-load, $1,100 front-load), those repairs usually favor replacement — especially front-load bearing jobs, which are labor-heavy.

How much does it cost to fix a washer that won’t drain?

A won’t-drain fault is usually the drain pump, about $150–$320 installed, or sometimes the water inlet valve at $120–$250. Both are DIY-friendly, though they involve water lines, so shut off the supply and watch for leaks.

Front-load or top-load — which lasts longer?

Premium top-load washers can reach 15–25 years, the longest of any washer, while premium front-loaders reach 15–20. At the budget tier they’re similar. Front-load bearing failures tend to be the more expensive repair.
