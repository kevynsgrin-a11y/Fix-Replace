Safety recalls

An active recall changes the whole calculation.

If your appliance is under an open federal recall, you may be entitled to a free repair or replacement. Before you pay a cent, we check — and if there’s a match, our advice is simple: pursue the free remedy.

How the check works

Enter your appliance’s UPC and we query the CPSC SaferProducts.gov Recall API — the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission’s official recall database. To keep it fast and resilient, we cache recall data at the edge and refresh it daily, so a lookup is instant and the economic verdict never has to wait on an external service.

1

You provide a UPC

The barcode number on your appliance. It’s used for the lookup and discarded — for anonymous users it’s never linked to you.

2

We match against CPSC records

We check the official recall database for an active recall covering your product.

3

A match reshapes the verdict

An active recall pulls the recommendation firmly toward “repair” — because the manufacturer, not you, typically owes the fix.

What an active recall means

Pursue the free remedy first

When a product is recalled, the manufacturer is generally responsible for making it right — at no cost to you — through a free repair, a replacement, or a refund. That’s almost always a better outcome than either a paid repair or buying new, so it takes priority over the economic math.

Concretely, if we find an active recall:

  • Stop before you pay. Don’t authorize a paid repair for the recalled defect until you’ve checked the remedy.
  • Contact the manufacturer or follow the official recall notice to claim your free repair, replacement, or refund.
  • Mind the safety guidance. Some recalls advise you to stop using the product until it’s remedied — follow the notice.

Why this matters for repair-or-replace

A recall isn’t just a safety issue — it’s a financial one. A defect that would otherwise cost you a repair bill (or push you toward an expensive replacement) may be fully covered. Folding the recall check into the decision means you don’t accidentally pay for something the manufacturer already owes you.

Coverage and limits

Recall data can be incomplete or delayed, and a “no match” result is not a guarantee that no recall exists. Always confirm directly with the manufacturer or at the official CPSC notice. Our recall check is an aid, not a legal determination — see the terms.
