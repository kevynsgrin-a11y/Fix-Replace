How it works

A real cost model — not a rule of thumb.

The tired "if the repair costs more than half a new unit, replace it" rule ignores energy, risk of re-failure, and the time value of money. We don't.

1

Describe the unit

Appliance, build tier, age, the failed part, and the repair quote. Add your metro or ZIP to localize labor and energy — or skip it for national averages.

2

We run the math

A Weibull model estimates remaining life; a net-present-cost model prices repairing-and-keeping against replacing — including energy, disposal, salvage, and the odds of a second failure.

3

Get an honest verdict

A clear repair-or-replace call with a confidence score, break-even point, safety flags, and a recall check. If your quote looks predatory, we say so.

FAQ

Common questions

How does it decide between repairing and replacing?
We compare the net present cost of repairing and keeping your current unit against buying new over the same horizon. That includes your quote, the risk of a follow-on failure, energy at local rates, disposal, and a salvage credit — all discounted to today's dollars. The full model is written up in how it works.
Is there a sign-up or paywall?
No. The calculator is free and needs no account or email. Partner links appear only after your verdict, are clearly labeled, and never influence the result.
What data does the tool use?
Appliance lifespans from NAHB and InterNACHI, regional labor rates from BLS OEWS (occupation 49-9031), energy rates from the EIA, and recall data from the CPSC SaferProducts database — each one cited on the methodology page. Rates are also broken out by metro area.
What happens if my repair quote looks too high?
If a quote is far outside the normal range for that repair, we withhold a definitive verdict and recommend a second opinion instead of showing false precision. A realistic number gets you a real verdict — the cost guides show the normal range per appliance.
Is this a substitute for a technician's diagnosis?
No — it's a financial and lifespan decision aid, not a safety certification or repair diagnosis. Always follow a licensed professional's guidance for gas, refrigerant, or high-voltage work.
