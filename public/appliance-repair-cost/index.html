Local repair costs

What a repair really costs where you live.

The same repair is priced very differently across the country, because the biggest variable — field labor — tracks the local wage market. We convert BLS area wages for appliance repairers into a localized cost band for each major metro.

How localized pricing works

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes an area mean hourly wage for Home Appliance Repairers (occupation 49-9031). We divide each metro’s mean by the national mean baseline of $24.10/hr to get a labor multiplier, then apply it only to the labor share of each repair — parts cost the same everywhere; labor does not. For reference, the national median wage is $22.68/hr and the 90th percentile is $34.93/hr.

The quality gate: no thin pages

We only publish and index a metro page when the underlying data supports a trustworthy estimate. Metros without sufficient wage granularity fall back to state or national rates in the calculator and are kept noindex rather than shipped as thin, low-value pages. This is the same no-fake-precision principle that governs the whole tool.

Why Miami and Boston aren’t the same

Put the extremes side by side: a repair that’s fairly priced in Miami (mean wage $22.87/hr) would look about a quarter more expensive in Boston (mean wage $29.60/hr) for the same labor. That’s not gouging — it’s the local wage market. A tool that ignores geography will mislabel fair Boston quotes as expensive and predatory Miami quotes as normal. Ours doesn’t.
