A “high” Boston quote may still be fair

Because Boston’s labor rate is roughly a quarter above the national average, out-of-town price charts will make fair local quotes look inflated. Our predatory-quote guard uses the Boston band, so it won’t wrongly flag a fair local price — and it will still catch a quote that’s genuinely double the regional norm. Frequently asked questions

How much does appliance repair cost in Boston?

Boston bands start at $117–$223 for a dryer thermal fuse and $135–$315 for an oven or range bake element, rise to $290–$522 for a washer drum bearing and $402–$632 for a washer transmission, and top out at $557–$1,393 for a refrigerator sealed-system leak. Each figure carries the ≈1.23× Boston labor multiplier on its labor share.

Why does Boston have the highest appliance repair prices of the metros indexed here?

The BLS area mean wage for appliance repairers (49-9031) in Boston is $29.60/hr, roughly a quarter above the $24.10 national mean, giving a ≈1.23× multiplier — the highest of our indexed metros. Only the labor share scales; parts are priced nationally, which is why the gap is proportionally smallest on parts-heavy jobs like the $221–$551 electronic control board.

My Boston repair quote looks high against a national price chart. Is it?

Probably not. National charts understate Boston because they average in metros paying far less per hour. Judge the quote against the Boston band instead: $172–$367 for a dishwasher or washer drain pump, $402–$632 for a washer transmission. Our predatory-quote guard uses the Boston band too, and flags a price only when it runs to about double it.

With Massachusetts electricity near 31 ¢/kWh, should I just replace an old appliance?

Not automatically. At roughly 31 ¢/kWh, a worn refrigerator wasting about 400 kWh a year costs on the order of $124/year more to run than a modern one, so energy strongly favors replacing an always-on unit. But Boston’s high labor rate doesn’t change the cheap faults: a $135–$315 bake element is still small next to the price of a new range.
